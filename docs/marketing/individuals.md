# Bring useful context into your next task

> Draft audience page for review.

You should not have to explain the same system landscape every time you start
a task, join a project or open an agent session. A maintained Context Fabric
view gives you a place to begin: the systems involved, where evidence lives,
and the limits of what is known.

## Start with one useful task

Try preparing a briefing, finding the systems involved in a change, or getting
oriented before a handoff. Share the [Start here guide](../../START-HERE.md),
your relevant documents or view, and a prompt like this:

> Help me prepare a briefing on [task or change]. Reuse relevant Context Fabric
> context if it exists. Show the systems involved, the evidence behind the
> explanation and the gaps I should resolve. If context is missing, recommend
> the simplest way to capture what this task needs.

You should leave with a grounded explanation and a clearer account of what is
known. When local authoring and validation are available, the agent can help
turn supporting facts into reusable context. Without those capabilities, you
can still read or draft; the result remains unvalidated. See a
[fictional view](../../views/meridian-health-agency/view.md) for an example.

**[Start with your task](../../START-HERE.md).** Reading and drafting require no
installation. You do not need a team-wide rollout to begin, and your private
paths and access settings stay separate from shared facts.

Think of this as the crawl step: useful context for the work in front of you.
When shared upkeep becomes useful, [bring your team in](teams.md).
